How to calculate the filament cost of a 3D print

Accurate 3D print material costing starts with the slicer estimate and the net plastic weight of your spool. Follow these steps to avoid spool-tare errors and account for supports, purges, and reprints.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Slice the model with your real layer height, infill, walls, supports, and brim or raft settings, then record the total filament weight in grams.
  2. Check the spool price (including shipping if you track landed cost) and the net filament weight printed on the box, typically 1,000 g or 750 g.
  3. Add a small extra percentage only for waste not already in the slicer total, such as purge towers, color swaps, or expected test prints.
  4. Optionally enable the electricity section and enter print duration in hours, average printer wattage, and your local kWh rate.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Spool price: Purchase price of one filament spool.
  • Net filament in spool: Filament mass only (commonly 1,000 g or 750 g), not the empty reel.
  • Slicer material weight: Total grams shown in Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer, or Cura.
  • Extra waste not in slicer: Optional buffer for purge, failed starts, or reprints.
  • Include electricity cost estimate: Adds estimated printer power consumption to material cost.
  • Print duration: Estimated print time from slicer in hours.
  • Average printer power draw: Typical desktop FDM printer averages 80 W to 180 W during printing.
  • Electricity rate per kWh: Your local utility price per kilowatt-hour.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Weighing a full spool on a kitchen scale without subtracting the empty reel (which often weighs 180 g to 260 g).
  • Double-counting support and purge weight when your slicer already includes them in the gram total.
  • Treating raw filament cost as a commercial selling price. Machine depreciation, failed prints, post-processing, and labor are separate business costs.
  • Assuming different filament materials have the same cost per meter. Density varies from ~1.04 g/cm³ (ABS) to ~1.27 g/cm³ (PETG).

Practical ordering checklist

  • Verify whether your slicer reports grams, meters, or both—grams give direct cost without needing filament density.
  • Measure average wattage with a plug-in meter if you include electricity; heated beds draw peak power mainly during warm-up.
  • Keep currency labels consistent across spool price and electricity rate.
