How to calculate the filament cost of a 3D print

Calculate the material cost of a print from its slicer weight and the net filament weight and price of a spool. Electricity, labor and machine wear are separate costs.

Quick answer: A $20, 1,000 g spool and a 50 g print cost $1.10 when 10% extra material is included.

Step by step

  1. Slice the model and read the estimated filament weight in grams from the slicer.
  2. Note the spool price and the net filament weight printed on the spool. The empty reel is not included.
  3. Add material the sliced file does not include, such as failed starts, test prints or reprinted parts.
  4. Divide the spool price by its net weight for the cost per gram, then multiply by the total grams.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Spool price
  • Net filament in spool
  • Slicer material weight
  • Material not included by slicer

How the calculation works

Divide spool price by net spool weight in grams to get cost per gram. Multiply by print weight and by one plus your extra-material percentage. Use the slicer total including supports and purge when available; only add an allowance for material that total excludes.

Common mistakes

  • Using the gross weight including the empty reel, which makes each gram look cheaper than it is.
  • Forgetting failed or reprinted parts when pricing a job.
  • Treating material cost as the full print cost. Electricity, machine wear and your time are separate.

Another worked example

A $30, 750 g spool and a 125 g slicer total cost $5.00 with no additional allowance.

What changes the estimate

Net spool weight means filament only, not the empty spool. The allowance is optional, not a recommended failure rate. Costs use the currency you select; the calculator does not convert exchange rates. This is material cost, not a selling price.
